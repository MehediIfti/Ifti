Founders buy speed, enterprises buy safety

Same proposal, two buyers. Each reads the first paragraph looking for one thing.

Founder
"How fast can I ship?"
Enterprise
"What happens if this goes wrong, and who gets blamed?"

Pitch speed to the enterprise and you sound reckless. Pitch governance to the founder and you sound slow.Scope, price and timeline barely change between the two. Only the framing does.

So I write the first paragraph twice. Everything below it stays the same.

N°00328 Sep 2026

Scope creep starts at kickoff

Most scope fights trace back to week one. Someone assumed something, and nobody said it out loud.

So I end every kickoff with one question.

"What would make you unhappy in month two, even if we hit every deliverable?"Ask the most senior person in the room, and ask them last. The answer changes when the boss is listening.

The answers are the real scope. Then three steps.

  • Write them down.
  • Put them in the recap.
  • Get a yes.

N°00214 Sep 2026

The recap email is the contract people read

Nobody rereads the statement of work. Everyone rereads the email you sent after the call.

So that email carries the weight. Keep it short, cut the adjectives, and cover four things.

  • Decisions. What we agreed.
  • Owners. One name per decision.
  • Dates. When each thing happens.
  • Open questions. What's still unresolved, and who answers it.

If the stakeholder can't forward it to their boss without editing, it isn't finished.Second test. Could the client's CFO read it cold and know what happens next?

Shelf

Books that changed how I work. One line each on why.

  • Never Split the Difference · Chris Voss

    Negotiation is mostly listening. Labels and calibrated questions belong in every pricing call.

  • The Mom Test · Rob Fitzpatrick

    How to ask questions people can't answer politely. Required reading before any discovery call.

  • High Output Management · Andrew Grove

    Treat work like a production line and find the bottleneck. Still the clearest book on management.

  • Thinking in Bets · Annie Duke

    Judge a decision by what you knew when you made it. The outcome is a separate question.

  • The Design of Everyday Things · Don Norman

    Good design explained in plain words. Useful when the audience is a CFO.

    Why they get better

    Every agent runs the same loop. Tap a step, or watch it go round.

    I send a message on Telegram: a receipt, a reminder, a call to prep for.

    It uses its tools to get it done: web search, a browser, vision, a terminal, and subagents that each work in their own sandbox.

    Once it has solved the problem, it writes down how as a reusable skill.

    Next time something similar comes up, it starts from that skill. That's why Plutus now files a receipt from a single photo.
