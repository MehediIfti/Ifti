N°00412 Oct 2026

Founders buy speed. Enterprises buy safety.

Same proposal, two buyers. A founder reads it asking, "How fast can I ship?" An enterprise stakeholder reads it asking, "What happens if this goes wrong, and who gets blamed?"

Pitch speed to the enterprise and you sound reckless. Pitch governance to the founder and you sound slow.The scope, price and timeline barely change. Only the framing does.

So now I write the first paragraph twice. Everything below it stays the same.

N°00328 Sep 2026

Scope creep starts at kickoff

Most scope fights aren't about scope. They're about an assumption nobody said out loud in week one.

I now end every kickoff with one question: "What would make you unhappy in month two, even if we hit every deliverable?"Ask it to the most senior person in the room, last. The answer changes when the boss is listening.

The answers are the real scope. Write them down, put them in the recap, and get a yes.

N°00214 Sep 2026

The recap email is the contract people read

Nobody rereads the statement of work. Everyone rereads the email you sent after the call.

So that email does the heavy lifting: decisions, owners, dates, open questions. Five lines, no adjectives.My test: could the client's CFO read it cold and know what happens next?

If the stakeholder can't forward it to their boss without editing it, it isn't done.

Work

Key Accounts at Musemind. I make sure design work pays for itself.

Musemind is a UX design agency working with companies across the GCC. My job is the relationship: understanding what the client's business needs, keeping stakeholders aligned, and steering the work toward outcomes rather than deliverables.

Who I work with

Enterprise
Teams that need design to get through procurement, internal politics and a dozen stakeholders, and still ship something good.
Founders
People who need a product that sells before the runway ends. Fewer meetings, faster decisions.

How I work

  • Outcomes before outputs. A screen is not a result. Revenue, retention or time saved is.
  • Say the hard thing early. Bad news gets more expensive every week you wait.
  • One owner per decision. If everyone owns it, nobody signs it off.
  • Write it down. If it isn't in the recap, it didn't happen.

Shelf

Books that changed how I work, with one line on why.

  • Never Split the Difference · Chris Voss

    Negotiation is mostly listening. Labels and calibrated questions changed how I run pricing calls.

  • The Mom Test · Rob Fitzpatrick

    How to ask questions that people can't answer politely. Essential for discovery calls.

  • High Output Management · Andrew Grove

    Treat your work like a production line and find the bottleneck. Still the best book on management.

  • Thinking in Bets · Annie Duke

    Judge a decision by the information you had, not by how it turned out.

  • The Design of Everyday Things · Don Norman

    Why I can explain to a CFO what UX actually means.
