# Voice

How everything on this site is written. New pages, notes, labels and button text all follow it.

The model is Paul Graham's essays: plain words, short sentences, claims as strong as they can be while staying true. Authoritative because it's specific, human because it's honest about what I don't know. It's my voice, not an imitation of his. I write from sales and partnerships, not from startup investing.

## The rules

1. **Ordinary words, simple sentences.** If a word would sound odd said out loud across a table, cut it. Use "use", not "utilise". Use "help", not "empower".
2. **Lead with the claim.** The first sentence of a paragraph says the thing. The reasons come after, usually in one clause: "because…", "so…".
3. **Strong, but true.** Make the claim as bold as it can be without becoming false. Then qualify it honestly with "usually", "often", "most", "I think". Qualifiers set the scope; they're not there to hedge everything.
4. **Concrete beats abstract.** Name the client type, the number, the meeting, the email. "45+ client accounts in about 15 countries" beats "extensive global experience".
5. **Say "I" when it's me.** Speak from what I've seen ("I've watched deals die in week two"). Use "you" when giving advice. Never "we believe" or "our team is passionate".
6. **Short lines land the point.** Mix medium sentences with short ones. End a paragraph on the shortest, sharpest line, not a summary.
7. **Ask the question the reader is asking.** "Why does this happen? Usually because…" A question is a fine way to turn a corner.
8. **Cut until it hurts, then stop.** Draft loose, edit tight. Most edits are deletions. If a sentence doesn't teach something, delete it.
9. **No hype, no fluff.** None of these: "delve", "leverage", "unlock", "seamless", "game-changer", "passionate", "synergy", "in today's fast-paced world", "tapestry", "elevate", "robust". No exclamation marks.
10. **Facts only.** Never invent a client, a quote, a number or a story. If I don't know it, it doesn't go on the page.

## Shapes that work

- **The flat opener.** "Most scope fights start at kickoff." Then explain.
- **The reversal.** "People think X. Actually Y." Use it sparingly, and only when Y is true.
- **The because.** "Founders buy speed because a slow quarter can kill them."
- **The admission.** "I don't know yet." / "I was wrong about this." It earns trust for the strong claims.
- **The closing line.** One short sentence that makes the reader see the point differently.

## Before and after

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| I'm passionate about building meaningful partnerships that drive value. | I turn first conversations into partnerships that last. |
| Leveraging my extensive experience across diverse markets… | I've worked in 35+ markets. Most problems look the same in all of them. |
| We believe communication is key to success. | The recap email is the contract people actually read. |
| Exciting new feature! Check it out! | It's live. Here's what changed. |

## Small text

- **Buttons** are verbs: "Book a call", "Read my notes", "Send".
- **Labels** are nouns, one to three words.
- **Captions** are one sentence, and they say something a glance wouldn't.
- **Errors** say what happened and what to do: "Couldn't send. Try again, or email me."

## Checklist before publishing

- Does the first sentence say the thing?
- Is every number real?
- Could a non-native English speaker read it in one pass?
- Did I cut at least one sentence?
- Does it sound like me talking to a smart friend, not like a brochure?
